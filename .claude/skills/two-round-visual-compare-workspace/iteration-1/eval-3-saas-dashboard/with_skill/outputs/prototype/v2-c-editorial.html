Revenue · Q2 2026

A quarter of quiet compounding, and one surprise at Atlas Brewing.

MRR crossed $184,000 on the strength of three enterprise expansions and a 38-seat net add. But the headline number hides the work of an unusually steady April and a May we did not see coming.

Maya Hollander · Head of Revenue · 12 min read · Updated 2 h ago

It is tempting, at the end of a quarter that closed up 6.2%, to write a triumphal note. The numbers are kind: $184,210 in MRR, 2,419 paying seats, 412 customers. But the more interesting story is the second derivative — the shape of the growth, not just its slope.

April was a textbook month. Net new MRR landed within 3% of the forecast we had set in March, and expansion revenue from existing customers outpaced new logo revenue for the eighth consecutive quarter. The mix of seats, plans, and add-ons drifted exactly as our cohort model predicted: large customers growing into their plans, mid-market customers holding flat, a long tail of trials converting at 42.8%.

May was the surprise. On the 14th, Atlas Brewing Co — a 12-seat customer we had written off as flat — requested a top-up of 8 seats to support a new taproom rollout. Two weeks later, they added 6 more, and the procurement email we received on the 31st read like a love letter to the product. We do not, as a rule, write customer love letters back. This one deserved a reply.

Atlas is not an outlier so much as a confirmation. The pattern of the quarter — a few large expansions, a steady drumbeat of mid-market renewals, and a long tail of trials that convert above industry baseline — is the pattern we have been working toward for four quarters. It is also the pattern that is hardest to defend in a board meeting, because it does not announce itself. The compounding shows up in the cell text, not in the headline.

None of this is to suggest that the quarter was without friction. The 0.3-point tick in net revenue churn is small but visible, and three of the four churned accounts cited the same cause: a missing CSV export that we have been promising since November. We have committed, in writing, in this issue, to ship it by the end of July. (Engineering is reading.)

The other thing worth naming, briefly, before we get to the dashboard: the team is tired. April was a sprint, May was two sprints, and the long Memorial Day weekend was earned. We have front-loaded June around rest, with the exception of a single feature — proration, finally — that has been on the roadmap for 142 days and is now, mercifully, in code review.
